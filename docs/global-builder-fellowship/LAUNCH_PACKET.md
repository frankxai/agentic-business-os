# Global Builder Fellowship — 90-day launch packet

**Status:** proposed, no external execution authority.

## Done condition for a first pilot

A pilot is ready to be proposed to Frank only when it has:

1. one country/community partner receipt that passes every condition in
   [COUNTRY_PARTNER_GATE.md](./COUNTRY_PARTNER_GATE.md);
2. a clear challenge co-authored with local people;
3. written, locally reviewed participant, data, IP, payment, and safeguarding
   terms;
4. an approved budget and payer of record;
5. a cohort cap of 24 or fewer and at least two independent reviewers per
   selected applicant;
6. a facilitator / steward staffing plan with named escalation owner;
7. a supporter boundary that gives no one applicant data or selection power by
   default;
8. a private, reversible operations plan and a post-cohort fairness review.

No amount of public interest, social reach, or investor enthusiasm substitutes
for these conditions.

## Phase 0 — prepare the right to invite (days 1–21)

| Workstream | Deliverable | Human gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local stewardship | Candidate steward map, conflicts, compensation expectation, and country-gate score | Frank approves outreach and any compensation |
| Listening | A small, consented listening protocol and a synthesis of local opportunity / harm signals | Privacy and local-partner review before contact |
| Challenge | One co-authored challenge brief with a clear non-goal and rights/safety boundaries | Program lead + steward approve |
| Participant protections | Plain-language draft terms, eligibility, data map, IP default, withdrawal and escalation process | Human legal/privacy/safeguarding review |
| Operations | Cohort cap, staffing, calendar, language access, support budget, payment route | Frank approves budget and any vendors |
| Measurement | Baseline fairness and proof metrics, review-agreement calculation, stop rules | Program lead approves |

**Deliverable:** a private `country-admission-receipt` and a go/no-go memo. If
the gate fails, publish nothing and continue local learning only if the people
involved want that.

## Phase 1 — Open Build Challenge (days 22–42)

| Week | Participant experience | Operating evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plain-language challenge brief, optional orientation, proof template, office hour | Terms acceptance, accessibility check, no sponsor data exposure |
| 2 | Build a smallest artifact; optional peer feedback | Proof packets, support tickets, steward incident log |
| 3 | Submission closes; participants choose what is private, shareable, or withdrawable | Immutable timestamp + consent state |

Every eligible participant gets a proof-plan output or useful feedback path.
The program may not market acceptance rate, finalist count, prizes, or demand
until each is true and approved.

## Phase 2 — review and acceptance (days 43–49)

1. Remove first-round identity and prestige signals where practical.
2. Assign two independent reviewers who disclosed conflicts.
3. Score using the published rubric; log rationale and disagreement.
4. Reintroduce only the information necessary for accessibility, safeguarding,
   and diversity / conflict checks.
5. Send clear outcomes and an appeal-for-process-error path.
6. Offer selected fellows terms before asking for any new private material.

**Selection cap:** 24. The cap exists to protect quality, steward attention,
and payment reliability. Do not expand it because a sponsor asks for volume.

## Phase 3 — six-week Build Fellowship (days 50–91)

| Week | Decision question | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What does the fellow want to make, for whom, and what must remain human-controlled? | Build contract + privacy/rights boundary |
| 2 | What is the first testable artifact? | Demo / prototype / research record |
| 3 | What happens in expected, edge, and blocked cases? | Evaluation cases + result log |
| 4 | What did a real, consented user or peer learn from it? | Feedback evidence, not vanity metrics |
| 5 | What is the viable next route: continue, redirect, pause, or refer? | Decision record + support boundary |
| 6 | What should become public, private, or opt-in for supporters? | Participant-controlled proof packet |

The fellowship uses the Foundry's Pathfinder and proof loops as tools. It does
not require a fellow to use a particular model, disclose personal data, or turn
their project into a Starlight product.

## Phase 4 — proof room and aftercare (days 92–105)

| Route | Conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Community / alumni | Default. Fellow retains access to peers, evidence templates, and optional future sessions. |
| Customer learning | Fellow opts in and a real potential user / pilot opportunity has separate terms. |
| Mentor introduction | Fellow requests it and can see the mentor's role / conflict first. |
| Supporter room | Fellow opts in to a private, non-binding display of a redacted proof packet. |
| Foundry design partner | Only when reusable asset, authority, support boundary, and relationship score pass. |
| Venture conversation | Separate human/legal conversation after demonstrated mutual contribution; never a prize or default next step. |

Within 14 days, run an anonymous or confidential participant retrospective:

- Did participants understand the terms and IP defaults?
- Was any payment late, unclear, or coercive?
- Did the selection process feel fair and explainable?
- Was any supporter behavior uncomfortable or inappropriate?
- Did language, location, disability, caregiving, or connectivity exclude
  anyone?
- Would participants recommend the program without a financial incentive?

Publish only aggregated, consented findings; correct any public claim that the
retrospective cannot support.

## Budget model (approval required)

Plan the first cohort by categories, not fictional exact price points:

| Budget category | Why it exists |
| --- | --- |
| Local steward compensation | Local insight and participant care are real work. |
| Fellow stipend / prize | Prevents participation from becoming an unpaid luxury. |
| Translation and access | Materials, live interpretation, captions, connectivity, disability access. |
| Mentors and reviewers | Independent evaluation and specialist help. |
| Tools / cloud access | Only approved, privacy-safe tools with clear cancellation / credit caps. |
| Legal, tax, privacy, and safeguarding | Required before real participation or payments. |
| Contingency and aftercare | Keeps promises after the demo day. |

No sponsor logo, public amount, or award promise is published before the exact
budget has a human approver and a workable payment route.

## High-confidence first hypothesis

The best first proof is not “can we attract thousands of applicants?” It is:

> With one trusted local steward and 24 or fewer fellows, can Starlight help
> people make stronger, more ethical proof artifacts than a normal hackathon,
> while participants report clear ownership, fair treatment, and a useful next
> path?

If the answer is no, do not scale demand. Fix the fellowship experience first.
