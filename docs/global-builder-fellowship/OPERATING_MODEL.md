# Global Builder Fellowship operating model

## Decision

Create a **Fellowship**, not a labor marketplace. The entry mechanism can be a
competition, but its purpose is discovery and a useful proof experience for all
participants—not extracting free work or manufacturing winner-take-all hype.

The program model is:

```text
local insight → open proof → paid build support → proof packet →
community / customer learning → consented supporter access → independent venture path
```

No arrow in that chain transfers IP, creates employment, promises investment,
or commits Starlight capital by default.

## Participant journey

| Stage | Timebox | Participant receives | Starlight decision | Evidence produced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local discovery | 2–3 weeks | Transparent brief, language/access route, eligibility and data terms | Whether a country and challenge are safe to open | Country gate receipt |
| Open Build Challenge | 2 weeks | A prompt, office hours, a proof template, and feedback path | Which submissions meet the published rubric | Problem statement + smallest proof |
| Blind review | 1 week | Outcome notice and constructive next step | Which applicants join the fellowship | Two independent rubric scores |
| Build Fellowship | 6 weeks | Stipend or tool/access support as separately approved, proof cells, coaching, evaluation cases | Which projects are ready for a showcase or further support | Demo, evidence log, risks, next experiment |
| Proof room | 1 week | Participant-controlled proof packet and privacy choices | Whether a project can be shown to supporters | Redacted public/private packet pair |
| Alumni / venture route | ongoing | Community, peer support, customer-learning and partner paths | Whether an individual invitation is appropriate | Consent, diligence, and relationship record |

## Open challenge design

The first challenge should ask for a response to a meaningful, locally observed
problem through one of three tracks chosen with local stewards:

1. **Useful work** — help a small organization or community perform an
   information, coordination, education, creative, or service task better.
2. **Local intelligence** — turn scattered local knowledge into an ethical,
   well-sourced tool, workflow, or product prototype.
3. **Creative technology** — create a culturally specific interactive, media,
   or learning experience with a clear audience and rights story.

Submissions need only include:

- the problem and who experiences it;
- why the applicant understands it;
- a small artifact or experiment;
- what would make it useful within 30 days;
- what needs human review, consent, rights, or safety work.

Do **not** ask for a full startup plan, viral video, polished English deck, or
unpaid bespoke work for a sponsor.

## Selection model

First-round selection is blind where practical. Names, school names, social
follower counts, country-specific prestige signals, and public polish are
separated from the reviewer packet. Each qualifying submission receives two
independent reviews.

| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Problem intimacy and usefulness | 30% | Direct knowledge of a real, bounded problem and who benefits. |
| Evidence of making | 25% | A test, prototype, research artifact, or concrete learning—not credentials. |
| Learning velocity | 20% | Ability to name uncertainty, seek feedback, and improve. |
| Safety, rights, and trust awareness | 15% | Honest handling of data, people, IP, and foreseeable harm. |
| Community contribution | 10% | Willingness to help peers without becoming unpaid program labor. |

The rubric explicitly excludes English accent, proximity to investors, ability
to self-fund, brand familiarity, and follower count. A participant can request
an explanation of their score and a re-review for process error.

## Fellowship delivery

Each cell has 3–5 fellows, one trained community steward, and an optional
technical mentor. A weekly rhythm:

| Beat | Purpose | Required artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday — intent | One hypothesis and one smallest safe action | One-sentence build contract |
| Midweek — show | Peer feedback on the actual artifact | Screenshot, demo, or field note |
| Friday — evidence | Compare expected, edge, and blocked cases | Proof receipt + learning note |
| Monthly — reflection | Decide build, redirect, pause, or refer out | Decision record |

Use Starlight Community cells and Vibeclubs-style focused sessions as operating
patterns. Do not claim an AI mentor has made a business, legal, medical,
employment, investment, or safety decision for someone.

## Technology architecture: start human-in-the-loop

Phase 0 is deliberately low-tech and privacy-minimal:

```text
Participant-owned proof packet (GenCreator Community)
        ↓ explicit consent
Private reviewer packet (pseudonymous identifier)
        ↓ two human reviewers
Fellowship workspace / proof cell (Starlight Communities rhythm)
        ↓ opt-in display setting
Private supporter brief (Investor OS format)
```

Do not build a platform before the first cohort proves the operational model.
If a product surface is later justified, use separate data domains:

| Data domain | Minimum rule |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant identity and contact | Separate from the blind reviewer packet; least-privilege access. |
| Consent | Versioned, timestamped, revocable, and scoped to each audience. |
| Submission / artifact | Private by default; public only on explicit participant choice. |
| Review | Immutable score and rationale history; reviewer conflict declaration. |
| Fellowship work | Participant workspace remains participant-controlled unless a separate collaboration agreement says otherwise. |
| Supporter interest | Opt-in only; never sold, scraped, or treated as an investment commitment. |

AI assistance may help a participant brainstorm, translate, structure a proof
packet, or find evaluation cases. It must not silently rank people, infer
socioeconomic status, make selection decisions, expose private submissions, or
send external messages.

## Supporter and investor community

The valuable role for entrepreneurs, operators, investors, and wealthy
supporters is **patronage and access**, not ownership of a talent pipeline.

| Mode | Allowed purpose | Not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mentor | Honest feedback, office hours, technical or commercial perspective | Recruiting unpaid labor or claiming participant IP |
| Customer / pilot partner | Explain a real problem and, if separately agreed, fund a scoped pilot | Turning a challenge into unpaid client work |
| Sponsor | Fund stipends, tools, access, or a transparent challenge | Selecting winners privately or receiving participant data by default |
| Supporter | Introduce relevant people, provide feedback, offer non-binding interest | Public investment solicitation, fee-for-introduction, or a deal promise |
| Investor | Review an explicitly consented private diligence packet | Any transaction without separate counsel, diligence, and approved terms |

The first supporter room is invite-only, educational, and private. It does not
pool money, receive commissions, match investments automatically, sell
securities, or use public claims about funding. Every participant can decline
the room without losing fellowship status.

## Fair economics and IP

1. **Prize / stipend:** set only after country-specific payment, tax,
   classification, and budget approval. A published amount must be real and
   payable on the stated terms.
2. **IP:** fellows retain it by default. Starlight receives no license except
   the narrow, revocable permission required to run the program and display
   material the participant elects to show.
3. **Paid work:** any customer pilot needs a separate scope, price, payment
   route, support boundary, and IP agreement. It cannot be smuggled into the
   fellowship terms.
4. **Venture path:** use the Foundry's relationship ladder—design partner,
   distribution partner, co-creator, then only optionally venture partner.
   A fellowship acceptance or an investor introduction creates no equity,
   royalty, revenue share, or employment obligation.
5. **Costs:** tools, cloud credits, travel, childcare, connectivity, and
   accessibility support are budget lines to approve, not hidden requirements
   participants absorb.

initiatives, but that is a reason to collaborate with local ecosystems rather

## Measurement and stop rules

Measure quality before growth:

- percentage of applicants who complete a useful proof packet;
- independent-review agreement and participant appeal outcomes;
- on-time support delivery and participant-reported fairness;
- number of validated artifacts, customer-learning conversations, and safe
  next experiments;
- volunteer / steward load and burnout signals;
- opt-in community continuation after the fellowship;
- introductions that were requested, accepted, and useful—not headline funding
  amounts.

Stop or pause if any of the following is true:

- no credible local steward or language/access plan;
- no reviewed payment or safeguarding path;
- participant terms imply IP transfer, unpaid sponsor work, or investment;
- fewer than two independent reviewers per selected submission;
- the program cannot protect private submissions and consent choices;
- public claims outrun verified activity or outcome evidence;
- the program becomes primarily a source of cheap labor rather than a source of
  participant-owned capability and ventures.
