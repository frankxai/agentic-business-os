# Country partner gate

## Purpose

The Fellowship must earn the right to operate in a country or community. A
country name in a strategy document does not grant that right. This gate avoids
parachuting in, translating only the landing page, or treating a population as
a source of underpriced labor.

## Gate: all conditions must pass

| Gate | Evidence required | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local steward | One compensated, conflict-disclosed steward with real local community credibility and authority to veto a harmful brief | Program lead + local steward |
| Community listening | At least ten compensated or otherwise fairly acknowledged listening conversations across potential participant profiles; no scraped lead list | Local steward |
| Language and accessibility | Primary-language challenge materials, accessible format, timezone plan, and interpretation for key sessions | Program operator |
| Problem legitimacy | One challenge brief authored or materially approved by local people who experience the problem | Local steward + reviewer |
| Fair selection | Local review perspective, public rubric, blind first round where practical, conflict policy, and appeal route | Review lead |
| Payment and tax | Locally reviewed legal/payment/classification route before promises are made | Human legal/finance owner |
| Safeguarding and privacy | Age eligibility, consent, data minimization, emergency/escalation route, and participant withdrawal policy | Human safety/privacy owner |
| IP and rights | Participant-first default, media permission, AI/data rights, and public showcase choices reviewed in clear language | Human legal/IP owner |
| Supporter boundary | Sponsor and supporter rules signed before they can see participant information or participate in judging | Program lead |
| Exit and continuity | What happens if Starlight pauses: community handoff, data deletion/retention, artifact access, and support commitments | Program lead |

## Japan, Philippines, and Indonesia: appropriate first questions

Do not assume these countries need the same program. The locally stewarded
discovery phase should answer:

| Country lane | First questions, not assumptions |
| --- | --- |
| Japan | What Japanese-language format and trusted local institution would make a proof fellowship useful rather than duplicative? Which skill, workforce, or creative-technology problem is locally chosen? |
| Philippines | Which regional incubator, university, community, or builder group wants a collaborative proof program? What connectivity, payment, and regional access constraints must shape it? |
| Indonesia | Which Bahasa Indonesia-first community or institution can co-design the challenge? What local data, cultural, payment, and scheduling practices must be respected? |

All three are legitimate research lanes because public institutions already run
digital-skills, entrepreneurship, or innovation programs. That fact is an
invitation to find complementary local partners—not a licence to claim need or
readiness without their input. See [SOURCE_REGISTER.md](./SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## Partner selection score

Score 0–5 for each criterion. A total below 32/40 or any red flag means no
cohort.

| Criterion | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Participant trust | Existing, accountable relationship with builders—not just audience reach. |
| Local problem insight | Can name who benefits, who may be harmed, and what not to build. |
| Operational reliability | Can host sessions, help with access, and maintain participant communication. |
| Equity and accessibility | Has a concrete plan for language, disability, geography, schedule, and economic barriers. |
| Independence | Can challenge Starlight and sponsors; not paid by a sponsor to steer winners. |
| Rights and safety literacy | Understands consent, youth safeguarding, IP, and escalation needs. |
| Ecosystem complementarity | Adds a bridge to a university, incubator, community, employer, or civic group without pretending to represent all of them. |
| Continuity | Can sustain an alumni cell or make a clean handoff after the cohort. |

### Red flags

- asks to receive applicant contacts, submissions, or IP as a condition of
  partnership;
- wants a preselected winner, sponsor deliverable, or recruitment funnel;
- cannot disclose conflicts, payment expectations, or data handling;
- treats participation as unpaid outsourced work;
- requires participants to surrender rights or make public claims;
- cannot offer a safe escalation path for harm, harassment, or fraud concerns.

## Approval receipt

No country can open until the program lead records:

```text
Country / community:
Local steward and conflict disclosure:
Challenge co-author(s):
Language and accessibility plan:
Payment / tax / classification reviewer:
Privacy / safeguarding reviewer:
IP / media rights reviewer:
Supporter boundary signed by:
Maximum cohort size:
Start and end dates:
Exit / continuity plan:
Human approver and date:
```

The receipt contains only the minimum private contact information needed for
operations. It is never published as a marketing proof object without consent.
