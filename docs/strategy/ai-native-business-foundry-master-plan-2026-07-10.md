# AI-Native Business Foundry — master plan

**Status:** proposed operating direction
**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Owner:** FrankX
**Canonical product:** `agentic-business-os`
**Commercial front door:** [frankx.ai/foundry](https://www.frankx.ai/foundry)

## Decision

Use **FrankX AI-Native Business Foundry** as the single front door for people who want to turn AI capability into a business, service, product, or governed operating system.

Do not create another umbrella brand. Agentic Income, Agentic Passive Income, Disruptive Passive Income, GenCreator, AI Architect Academy, and Starlight remain specialist acquisition, learning, delivery, or community substrates.

The transformation is:

> tool user → proof builder → systems operator → AI-native founder → portfolio architect

The first public wedge is deliberately narrower:

> **Build and deploy one useful AI-enabled service in seven days.**

No income guarantee is implied. The first proof is a working artifact and a bounded pilot plan, not revenue.

## Customer promise

Turn one process the buyer understands into:

1. a human-approved outcome;
2. the smallest useful agent or workflow;
3. a demonstrable artifact;
4. a clearly scoped offer;
5. a reusable repository with instructions, tests, gates, and memory;
6. an optional recurring update, support, or licensing layer after proof.

## Five customer routes

| Route | Starting point | First proof | Best next offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student | Unsure which role or business to pursue | One deployed agent solving a narrow problem | Launch Kit |
| Restart | Existing expertise after job disruption | One former job process reframed as a service demo | Foundry Sprint |
| Founder | Idea without validated offer or architecture | Interview notes, offer page, demo, pilot plan | Business OS Pack |
| Agency | AI demand but inconsistent delivery | Repeatable client-delivery repo and bounded pilot | Operator License |
| SME | Too many tool choices and unclear risk | One measured workflow with a human gate | Implementation Sprint |

## Proof-to-business loop

1. Name one expensive, slow, repetitive, or trust-sensitive process.
2. Define the outcome and the human approval boundary.
3. Capture the current baseline and the facts the system may use.
4. Select the lightest runtime that can produce the outcome.
5. Build one demonstrable flow with evaluation cases.
6. Package the flow as a fixed-scope offer.
7. Run a bounded pilot with explicit stop conditions.
8. Convert what worked into a skill, agent profile, template, runbook, and proof packet.
9. Add recurring operations or licensing only when support and economics are understood.

## Community design

Use **Foundry Circle** as the provisional public name for the practice layer. Treat final naming as a human brand decision.

Members enter with an artifact and one unresolved decision. They do not enter through a blank introduction. Work happens in 3–5 person proof cells with one Friday proof object:

- fork or install receipt;
- deployed demo;
- evaluation result;
- offer page;
- pilot brief;
- retrospective and updated memory.

Avoid shame-based leaderboards. Reward repeatable shipping, honest retrospectives, useful reviews, and improvements contributed upstream.

## Compounding asset model

Every real implementation should produce public-safe reusable assets:

- one process pattern;
- one agent/skill contract;
- one architecture decision;
- one evaluation case;
- one deployment recipe;
- one claims/security lesson;
- one customer-safe proof artifact;
- one upstream improvement proposal.

Private facts and customer data remain in the instance. Generic machinery returns upstream through readable pull requests. This is the existing `HARNESS.md` principle: **brand is yours, machinery is shared**.

## Founder story

The defensible story is not “AI makes money automatically.” It is:

> Enterprise AI and AI CoE work taught Frank that architecture, governance, and production rigor matter. Building creator systems showed that reusable workflows compound. Installing those patterns for friends and family revealed that the same substrate can help ordinary operators own a useful system instead of renting another tool. The Foundry makes that translation teachable, forkable, and implementable.

Friend and family kits are artifact proof until permissioned business outcomes exist. Say what was built and verified; do not invent transformation or revenue claims.

## Success metrics

Measure activation before revenue:

- time to first useful artifact;
- Pathfinder-to-selected-route completion;
- fork-to-build rate;
- build-to-deploy rate;
- proof-packet completion;
- first pilot conversation;
- pilot-to-paid conversion;
- weekly active builders;
- implementation support hours per install;
- update adoption and renewal.

## Human gates

Explicit approval remains required before public pricing changes, checkout activation, production deployment, DNS, credentials, billing, customer claims, external messages, partner promises, licensing terms, or brand identity changes.
