# Fair selection rubric

Use this rubric for the blind first round. Score each criterion 1–5, multiply
by its weight, and record a short rationale tied to evidence in the submission.
At least two independent reviewers score each selected applicant.

| Criterion | Weight | Strong evidence | Do not use as a proxy |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Problem intimacy and usefulness | 30% | The applicant understands a real person/process and can name a bounded benefit. | School prestige, social following, a famous problem statement. |
| Evidence of making | 25% | A test, prototype, research artifact, or honest first experiment. | Production polish, expensive tools, design trend fluency. |
| Learning velocity | 20% | Can name uncertainty, seek feedback, and update a choice. | Confidence, charisma, perfect writing. |
| Safety, rights, and trust | 15% | Notices consent, data, IP, people, and likely harm. | Legal jargon or generic AI disclaimers. |
| Community contribution | 10% | Willing to give focused peer feedback and respect others' boundaries. | Existing audience size or willingness to work for free. |

## Required reviewer controls

- Blind identity, affiliation, follower count, and ability-to-pay signals where
  practical.
- Disclose and recuse conflicts before scoring.
- Calibrate on 2–3 practice submissions before the live pool.
- Preserve a score/rationale record and disagreement note.
- Reintroduce identity only for access, safeguarding, conflict, or genuinely
  necessary eligibility checks.
- Offer an appeal for process error; do not promise a score re-argument.

## Minimum fairness checks

Before selecting, confirm:

1. the weights total 100;
2. no reviewer is paid by a sponsor to pick winners;
3. every selected applicant has two scores;
4. every applicant received the same core brief and deadline accommodations;
5. no participant needs to sign away IP, publicity, or investment rights to
   enter;
6. the cohort cap matches actual steward and reviewer capacity.
