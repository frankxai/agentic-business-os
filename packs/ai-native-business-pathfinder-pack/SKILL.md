# AI-Native Business Pathfinder

Select one route and one proof. Avoid idea lists.

## Inputs

Collect only what changes the route:

- experience or domain knowledge;
- target buyer or people they can reach;
- one slow, expensive, repetitive, or trust-sensitive process;
- available time for seven days;
- technical comfort;
- data sensitivity and regulated territory;
- preference for service, product, or recurring operations.

If the user cannot name a process, ask for three tasks people repeatedly ask them to do. Choose the task with the clearest buyer, observable outcome, and safe access to facts.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the process.** State the current trigger, work, human decision, output, and failure cost.
2. **Choose the route.** Select exactly one: student, restart, founder, agency, or SME.
3. **Choose the business model.** Prefer one: productized implementation, vertical workflow pack, managed automation, skill/MCP product, micro-SaaS, content/affiliate system, or advisory/CoE sprint.
4. **Define the smallest proof.** Require a working artifact plus evaluation cases. Revenue is not the first proof.
5. **Select the architecture.** Read [references/route-matrix.md](references/route-matrix.md). Record the chosen route and at least one rejected alternative.
6. **Set human gates.** Publishing, messaging, spending, production, pricing, legal claims, and destructive actions remain human-approved.
7. **Write the seven-day plan.** Use [references/proof-plan-template.md](references/proof-plan-template.md).
8. **Return one next action.** Do not offer a menu of equal CTAs.

## Quality gate

The result must include:

- one buyer and one process;
- evidence or a plan to collect evidence;
- one human-approved outcome;
- one proof artifact;
- one architecture route;
- three evaluation cases: expected, edge, blocked;
- a support boundary;
- a seven-day plan;
- a reusable-asset extraction step;
- claims clearly labeled as fact, assumption, or hypothesis.

## Refuse or redesign

- income or ROI guarantees;
- fear-based job-replacement claims;
- unconsented scraping, likeness, or voice use;
- regulated personal advice without qualified review;
- unattended publishing, outreach, spend, or production mutation;
- always-on agents without heartbeat, audit log, least privilege, caps, owner, and rollback;
- a swarm when one agent plus tools can produce the proof;
- a white-label offer without explicit commercial rights, support, security, and downstream obligations.

## Output

Return these headings:

1. `Selected route`
2. `Process and buyer`
3. `Evidence and assumptions`
4. `Offer hypothesis`
5. `Proof artifact`
6. `Architecture decision`
7. `Human gates and risks`
8. `Seven-day build plan`
9. `Reusable assets created`
10. `One next action`
